## Policy chronology and source definitions

| Date | Publication | Analytical scope |
|---|---|---|
| 12 May 2023 | Gazette 48589, Notice 3407 | Proposed five-year targets. Use the national BLACK column and the same gazette’s observed `Workforce Profile 2022 (All employers)`. |
| 1 February 2024 | Gazette 50058, Notice R. 4295 | Intermediate consultation draft, retained for chronology. |
| 15 April 2025 | Gazette 52514, Notice 6124 | Final five-year targets effective from publication, using designated-group coverage. |
| 15 April 2025 | Gazette 52515, Notice 6125 | Administrative regulations, including EEA2 reporting, EEA12 planning and EEA17 classifications. |

In the May 2023 draft, BLACK combines African, Coloured and Indian. Male and female are contributions to the corresponding sector’s full occupational level. For example, the Agriculture Top Management National cells are 20.0% male and 15.0% female, with a total of 35.0%. The published BLACK cell takes precedence over a sum of rounded components. The 2022 baseline inherits the source’s All employers label. The management tables supply percentages without counts or a foreign-national column. They do not establish the coverage of all employers nationally or permit a count-based reconstruction. The Agriculture Top Management profile reports 16.1% BLACK and 83.1% White. The table does not allocate the difference to a published group. [May 2023 draft, printed pp. 21–22](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202305/48589gon3407.pdf)

The April 2025 final targets use designated groups. These include Black people, women and persons with disabilities who meet the citizenship conditions, including citizens by birth or descent and qualifying naturalised citizens. The female target can therefore include qualifying White women. Section 3.1 explains why the target shares need not sum to 100%, referring to White men without disabilities and foreign nationals. The EEA2 and EEA12 workforce templates treat foreign nationals as separate cells within the full level total. These support an inclusive workforce denominator for a **descriptive** CEE proxy, with foreign nationals excluded from its numerator. The Gazette does not provide an independent numerical bridge from CEE citizenship categories to statutory eligibility. [Final notice, printed pp. 5–10](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202504/52514gon6124.pdf) · [Administrative regulations, regulation 9 and EEA2/EEA12](https://ee.labour.gov.za/DMISO/Employment%20Equity%20Regulations.pdf)

The final tables publish a separate 3% disability target across sectors. It is outside the analytical extract and should not be added to the Top or Senior Management totals. The five-year milestones inform employers’ own annual EE targets. Regulation 9 describes the assessment against annual targets and the role of reasonable grounds. The sector percentages are policy benchmarks. Employer compliance requires evidence beyond these datasets. [Final notice, printed pp. 5–10](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202504/52514gon6124.pdf) · [Administrative regulations, printed pp. 8–9](https://ee.labour.gov.za/DMISO/Employment%20Equity%20Regulations.pdf)

## Baseline timing and instructions for R

1. For May 2023, filter targets to `draft_2023` and pair with the contemporaneously published 2022 gazette profile on `sector_id`, `occupational_level` and `gender`. Carry the BLACK population definition into the output. Use the original percentages where an independently rounded component sum differs.
2. For April 2025, the latest publicly available CEE workforce year was **2023**. A government Annual Performance Plan dates the 24th report's release to 24 June 2024. The 25th report's PDF edition is dated 16 May 2025 and was public by 21 May. The exact initial posting day is unconfirmed. Use workforce year 2024 only as a retrospective comparison. [24th report release evidence](https://www.dwypd.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/DWYPD_APP_25_0304.pdf#page=22) · [25th annual report](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Reports/Annual%20Reports/Employment%20Equity/2025/25th%20CEE%20Annual%20Report%20Final%2016%20May%202025%20%28Website%29%20%283%29.pdf) · [Public response to the 25th report](https://bmfonline.co.za/media-release/pace-of-equity-and-inclusion-in-employment-just-not-fast-enough/)
3. Present the April 2025 targets separately in the primary results. A labelled race-by-sex proxy is suitable as supplementary descriptive context. In `cee_management_representation_raw.csv`, filter the selected workforce year, `geographic_scope == "Sector"`, `reporting_population == "All reporting employers"` and the two management levels. Join the year's original CEE label to `ee_sector_reference.csv`. Within each sector and level, use one `total_employees` denominator for both sexes including Foreign national counts. Calculate the male numerator from African, Coloured and Indian male counts and the female numerator from African, Coloured, Indian and White female counts. Divide each numerator and their sum by the same denominator and multiply by 100. All 72 sector-level-year groups reconcile. The numerator omits qualifying White men with disabilities and does not establish legal citizenship eligibility. The 24th report repeats disability rows on three sector pages and some counts exceed corresponding all-employee counts. Exclude those disability rows from adjustments. Any target-minus-proxy difference is descriptive and must not be used for attainment or compliance. [Independent verification and R filters](supporting_independent_review.md#2025-final-targets-and-cee-proxy)
4. Keep the EE/CEE reference at the identified 18-sector scope. In both CEE reports, the trade body-table rows use the gazette-style trade and repair label. Their complete Top and Senior Management percentage vectors match the Appendix C sector headed “WHOLESALE TRADE, COMMERCIAL AGENTS AND ALLIED SERVICES”. The reference retains the original wording in each source.
5. Use QLFS for national industry employment context. Keep `national_total` separate from the ten industry series. Use the source vintages and comparability flags in tables or model inputs. QLFS industry employment is a survey estimate of employed persons and is unsuitable as a CEE management or B-BBEE ownership denominator. Keep the classifications independent.

## QLFS provenance and comparability

The preferred historical attachment and the 2024 fallback could not be downloaded as files. The paired-year fallback provides full coverage from Table B in five Stats SA Q3 releases. Preserved publisher-authored PDFs come from UCT DataFirst and Eastern Cape Provincial Treasury. The earliest and latest years in each pair share their release vintage. Both archived copies of the 2025 release agree on the Table B values on printed page 3, and those cells also match the publisher PDF as indexed. The intervening official Q3 2024 release confirms all Q3 2023 and Q3 2024 estimates at printed precision. Earlier observations have mixed release vintages. Differences with workbook estimates remain unverified. [Stats SA Q3 2025 release listing](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P0211&SCH=74048&page_id=1854) · [Eastern Cape Treasury copy](https://www.ectreasury.gov.za/upload/2025/stats/P02113rdQuarter2025.pdf) · [UCT DataFirst copy](https://www.datafirst.uct.ac.za/dataportal/index.php/catalog/1118/related-materials)

The series covers employed persons aged 15–64 in the household sample. Coverage includes private dwellings and workers’ hostels, with other institutional populations generally excluded. QLFS uses survey design, non-response adjustments and calibration to population estimates. For Q3 2020 and 2021, telephone interviewing limited the realised sample. Stats SA adjusted for telephone non-coverage. Mainly in-person interviewing resumed in 2022. The 2023 Q3 technical notes still describe raking to bias-adjusted telephone coverage controls, while the 2025 Q3 notes describe calibrated household weights. Retain the published weights through the published estimates. [Stats SA Q3 2021 release](https://datafirst.uct.ac.za/dataportal/index.php/catalog/888/download/12154) · [Stats SA Q3 2022 introduction](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0211/P02113rdQuarter2022.pdf) · [Stats SA Q3 2023 release](https://www.datafirst.uct.ac.za/dataportal/index.php/catalog/949/download/12570)

Stats SA revised the questionnaire in Q3 2025 to implement updated international standards for work, status in employment and informality. The release reports unchanged measurement and definitions for employment and unemployment. It identifies a break in the formal/informal categories. Table B retains the ten broad industry groupings, with the older label “Finance and other business services” in 2017. No broad industry classification break is identified in the selected tables. Treat 2025 as a methodology flag within the broad-industry series. Utilities combines electricity, gas and water. Trade includes wholesale, retail, repairs, hotels and restaurants. Finance includes financial intermediation, insurance, real estate and business services, so its scope exceeds CEE Financial and Insurance Activities. [Stats SA questionnaire announcement](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?p=18656) · [Q3 2025 release, printed pp. 1 and 3](https://www.ectreasury.gov.za/upload/2025/stats/P02113rdQuarter2025.pdf)

## Reproduction

Run from the checkout with Python 3.11+ and Poppler’s `pdftotext` and `pdfinfo` on PATH.

```sh
sh analysis/part_b/scripts/supporting/reproduce_supporting.sh
```

The command reads preserved PDFs, existing CEE inputs and the verification inputs in `analysis/part_b/scripts/supporting`. It validates source hashes and regenerates all five CSVs offline. The draft transcription and verification records preserve the scan-dependent work, so reproduction does not require OCR packages.
