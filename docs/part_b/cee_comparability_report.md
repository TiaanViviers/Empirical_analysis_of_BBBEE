# Comparability — preliminary

The 2016–2018 reports use 11 broad sectors. The 2019–2025 sector appendices use 18 sectors. Labels changed in some cases even within the latter period, including transport and wholesale; source labels alone do not establish equivalent definitions. The crosswalk preserves all 159 original year/sector labels and leaves standardized mappings empty pending classification research. Do not disaggregate or aggregate sector percentages. A defensible mapping and, where justified, count-weighted aggregation remain to be developed.

The 26th report identifies the amended designated-employer definition effective 1 January 2025: employers below 50 are no longer designated based on turnover; organs of state and employers designated through collective agreement remain exceptions. Reports fall from 29,269 in 2024 to 15,090 in 2025, employees covered from 7,699,665 to 6,896,041. The dataset does not identify a comparable employer subset, and the original reports have not been fully investigated for one. Do not interpret raw changes as transformation without accounting for reporting population changes. These are reports, not necessarily unique employers.

The data are a changing population of reporting employers, not a balanced employer panel or a representative census of the economy. Occupational definitions, temporary employee definitions, revisions, foreign-national treatment and public/private composition require further review. Exact publication dates are unverified. A 2016–2024 primary series remains a research target rather than a validated comparable series.

## Subsequent investigation

The official 2025 administrative regulations include EEA17 SIC-based descriptions, but do not prove historical employer mapping. At present detailed 2019–2024 trends are candidates for review, contingent on source-label verification and the 2019 count discrepancy. National 2016–2024 trends mix a retrospective 2016 percentage-sum measure with count-based subsequent years and changing reporting populations. A defensibly harmonised broad sector series cannot yet be recommended. Searches found employer-size breakdowns for DG notifications, not suitable management workforce breakdowns for a consistent 50-plus subset; see `cee_issue_register.md`.

The independently checked national management observations for 2016–2024 and detailed sector observations for 2019–2024 are suitable for the qualified descriptive uses and exact filters in `cee_independent_review.md`. This supersedes earlier statements that these series have not undergone independent source checking, while retaining all stated population and source-discrepancy caveats.
