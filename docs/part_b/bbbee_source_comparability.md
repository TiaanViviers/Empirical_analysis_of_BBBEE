# B-BBEE source and comparability audit

The recommended primary source is the Commission’s 2026 retrospective, using the 2016–2023 part of its 2013–2023 retrospective record. It describes verification dates and certificate/report entries, not the underlying company financial year or a census. The 2022 status report is a separate, supplementary portal series, not a continuation or revision of this series. Sources, hashes and physical pages are in `bbbee_source_inventory.csv` and `bbbee_data_availability.csv`.

## Retrospective methodology

Physical PDF pages 46–50 (printed 44–48): 56 of 89 SANAS agencies supplied data, supplemented by independently submitted certificates/reports; participation was voluntary and selective. Some agencies submitted templates and some documents were captured by the Commission. A year relates to the verification/certificate record; the exact temporal assignment algorithm is not specified. The 28,131 entries are not 28,131 unique firms. Duplicate removal first used entity number and year, then source-specific identifiers; missing identifiers were replaced by agency/year/sector codes. This procedure does not establish a reliable longitudinal identifier. Incomplete identifiers, heterogeneous inputs and confidentiality prevent reconstructing a balanced firm panel.

Inputs were standardised; invalid, blank and erroneous entries were flagged and excluded unless verified and corrected; out-of-range values were capped at applicable maxima. The report calls the ownership figures averages but does not specify weighting or indicator-specific valid denominators: do not infer either. The overall table 1 sample is contextual, not an ownership denominator. Across all years 11,109 entries are large, 9,599 QSE, 2,476 EME and 4,947 unknown size. Some regulated sectors require agency verification of EMEs. Sector codes are not SIC industries: Generic is a code category, not an industry. Many sector-year sample sizes are very small; for example Financial Services has 4 entries in 2013 and 9 in 2016, not necessarily the numbers used for each average.

Voting rights, female voting rights, economic interest and female economic interest are four separate percentage measures, not net realised ownership or the value of national wealth. Female averages greater than overall averages can result from differing valid populations or errors; denominators are absent and the reason is unresolved. Financial Services table 81 has this pattern for voting and economic interest in 2015, voting in 2016 and economic interest in 2020. Preserve these values and flag them. The report itself flags the 2013 Financial Services voting outlier. The repeated annual cross-sections cannot establish population trends or causal effects.

## 2022 status report

PDF pages 13–14 and 18–21 describe distinct datasets: 1,475 voluntary verification agency portal uploads in 2022 with unknown eligible universe, 155 JSE reports received and 141 accepted, and 95 public sector/SETA reports received and 77 accepted. The methodology also says 260 total and 225 analysed; 155+95=250 and 141+77=218, so these totals conflict and must not be silently reconciled. It says about 400 JSE listed entities and 402 public entities were eligible; populations fluctuate and are not the portal denominator. The portal ownership charts belong to the portal section; indicator-specific observations and weighting were not established.

Statement 100 describes voting rights, economic interest and net value realisation as distinct ownership indicators, but Figure 8’s reported black ownership is not explicitly mapped to either retrospective measure, nor is its weighting documented. Retain the source label. Figure 5 differs from Figure 8 for several historical black ownership values; use Figure 8 only for the specified pilot, preserve the conflict and do not splice. Figure 7 and 10 printed Generic zeros in 2017–18 are expressly not analysed and must be null analytically. Table 10 and Figure 11 are points or percentage achievement of points, not ownership shares. JSE Table 20’s caption says 2021 while its heading and surrounding text specify 2022: preserve the discrepancy.

## Changes in rules and interpretation

The 2013 Act amendment and generic amended Codes effective May 2015 alter the classification and scoring regime; the retrospective warns 2013 ownership is unusual and likely selected. Sector code dates differ: MAC effective 2016, Defence 2019, CA repealed 2016; financial sector amendments took effect in 2018. Nominal size thresholds and enhanced recognition/automatic status arrangements vary by sector, period and ownership; preserve source size and status, and check the contemporary applicable code before making cross-regime legal interpretations. The report mentions EMEs at R10m or less in its sample, not a verified universal historical sector threshold. No retrospective reclassification of Levels 1–8 is justified. Levels 5–8 are contributor levels, not equivalent to the separately published non-compliant category. The published count and percentage discrepancy in 2018 is documented in the pilot report.

No verified public CSV, workbook, or microdata was located in the bounded publisher/archive check. No firm-level inference, standard errors or economy-wide wealth claims follow from these sources.
