Use QLFS for national employment context. The review found no numerical extraction, calculation or mapping defect in the four datasets. It corrected the documentation on CEE release timing, the disability evidence and QLFS provenance.

## 2023 draft and 2022 workforce profile

The same notice publishes the national five-year proposal and the 2022 profile on the same sector, occupation, sex and BLACK basis. BLACK combines African, Coloured and Indian. Male and female shares each use the full corresponding occupational level. The national proposal is the benchmark for this comparison. The draft describes separate national and provincial EAP choices for employers, and the published profile is labelled “All employers” without reporting counts, coverage details or a separate foreign-national column. Interpret the comparison as the distance from the published profile to the proposed benchmark. The source supports neither an employer-level attainment rate nor a headcount shortfall. [Gazette 48589, folios 21–56](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202305/48589gon3407.pdf#page=3)

For R, select `policy_version == "draft_2023"`, `geographic_scope == "national"` and `target_population == "black"`. Select `reporting_year == 2022` and `population_group == "black"` in the gazette profile. Include Top and Senior Management and join one-to-one on `sector_id`, `occupational_level` and `gender`. Compute `target_pct - observed_pct` in **percentage points**. Positive values are shortfalls against the proposal, zero means equality at printed precision and negative values mean the published profile was above the proposal. For example, the Agriculture Top Management total gap is 35.0 − 16.1 = **18.9 percentage points**. Use `gender == "total"` for sector totals and the individual gender rows for sex-specific contributions.

The 108 published Male/Female/Total triplets across the draft, workforce profile and final targets reconcile exactly at published precision. Retain the published BLACK cell when rounded components differ. The Manufacturing Top Management national male BLACK target is 25.0% while the displayed components add to 24.9%. The Agriculture Top Management workforce profile shows 16.1% BLACK and 83.1% White. The remaining 0.8 points cannot be assigned to a population from that table. Preserve the reported shares without renormalising them. The February 2024 consultation draft supplies policy chronology and is outside the paired comparison. ## 2025 final targets and CEE proxy

The final notice covers suitably qualified people from designated groups. The statutory population is the union of Black people (African, Coloured and Indian), women and persons with disabilities who are South African citizens by birth or descent, or who naturalised before 27 April 1994, or naturalised after 26 April 1994 and would have qualified before that date had apartheid policies allowed it. This includes qualifying White women and qualifying White men with disabilities. A person can meet several criteria and contributes once to the union. Foreign nationals are outside the target numerator. The EEA2 and EEA12 workforce profile tables include foreign nationals in separate columns within the full occupational-level total, which is the appropriate comparison denominator. The gazette publishes a separate 3% disability benchmark across all sectors, and the EEA12 analysis template expresses it against the total workforce. Adding 3% to a management target or adding all disability cases to a race-by-sex numerator would double count overlapping groups. [Statutory citizenship definition](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Acts/Employment%20Equity/Amendment/Ammended%20Employment%20Equity%20Act%20gazetted%20on%20the%2016%20January%202014.pdf) · [Final notice, §§2–3 and folios 6–10](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202504/52514gon6124.pdf) · [Administrative regulations, EEA2, EEA3, EEA12 and EEA13](https://ee.labour.gov.za/DMISO/Employment%20Equity%20Regulations.pdf)

The CEE inputs can describe the combined share of African, Coloured and Indian men and African, Coloured, Indian and White women among the reporting employers' managers. The proxy excludes reported foreign nationals from its numerator and omits qualifying White men with disabilities. The CEE count data cannot independently establish qualifying citizenship for individuals in its race-by-sex cells. The proxy therefore has no established error bound or direction of error relative to the full eligible population.

The 24th CEE report repeats the same disability count vectors for Top and Senior Management in Administrative and Support, Agriculture, and Water/Waste. Some cells exceed the corresponding all-employee counts, including 388 White men with disabilities against 171 White men in Water/Waste Top Management. Rendered pages and text extraction agree. The report does not identify which sector, if any, those repeated vectors describe. The anomaly is in the separate disability tables. The 72 all-employee sector-level-year rows used for the proxy reconcile to their denominators. Preserve the source and exclude the repeated disability rows from any adjustment. No disability rows enter the supporting outputs. [24th CEE report, Appendix C PDF pp. 83, 84 and 98](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Reports/Annual%20Reports/Employment%20Equity/2024/24th%20Commission%20for%20Employment%20Equity%20Annual%20Report.pdf#page=83)

For R, use the following filters and calculations.

- For the published targets, select `policy_version == "final_2025"`, `geographic_scope == "national"`, `target_population == "designated_groups"` and the two extract management levels. Use the published `target_pct` and select the requested gender row.
- For supplementary context, use `cee_management_representation_raw.csv`. Select `reporting_year == 2023`, `geographic_scope == "Sector"`, `reporting_population == "All reporting employers"` and `occupational_level %in% c("Top management", "Senior management")`. Join the source label `sector_original` to `cee_2023_sector_original` in the reference. For retrospective context repeat for 2024 with `cee_2024_sector_original`, separately labelled. Map occupational-level and sex strings explicitly before comparing with targets.
- Within each sector and level, require ten unique sex-by-group count cells, one positive `total_employees` shared by the cells and equality between their count sum and that total. The denominator includes both foreign-national cells. Calculate `100 * numerator / total_employees` using `employee_count`. Use African, Coloured and Indian men for the male numerator, African, Coloured, Indian and White women for the female numerator, and their sum for the total. The two sex-specific measures share the full-level denominator. Keep the input precision until display.
- Label any signed difference `target_pct - proxy_pct` as a **difference from the CEE race-by-sex proxy** in percentage points. Omit binary attainment labels, rankings of statutory shortfalls, compliance rates and cross-policy changes in the size of the gap. The 2023 and 2025 benchmarks cover different populations. Regulation 9 provides for employer-level annual plans, applicable EAP and reasonable grounds in compliance assessment. Aggregate CEE counts cannot resolve these. [Administrative regulations, reg. 9](https://ee.labour.gov.za/DMISO/Employment%20Equity%20Regulations.pdf#page=8)

## QLFS provenance and comparability

Each preserved source has the Stats SA P0211 cover and matching report quarter. The official release listings confirm all five release dates. UCT DataFirst identifies Stats SA as producer and lists the matching archived statistical releases. For 2025, the Eastern Cape Treasury and DataFirst copies have distinct file hashes. Their printed Table B values agree with each other and with the publisher's indexed PDF, for all 22 observations. For 2017–2023 the review confirmed catalogue provenance and the local release contents. A fresh publisher binary comparison was unavailable for those four releases. The independent local hashes, byte lengths and page counts matched the source inventory. [Stats SA releases for 2017](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P0211&SCH=6814&page_id=1854), [2019](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P0211&SCH=7621&page_id=1854), [2021](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P0211&SCH=72945&page_id=1854), [2023](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P0211&SCH=73573&page_id=1854) and [2025](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P0211&SCH=74048&page_id=1854) · [2025 publisher PDF, Table B](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0211/P02113rdQuarter2025.pdf#page=8) · [Source inventory](supporting_source_inventory.csv)

The Table B fallback gives complete Q3 coverage at a consistent seasonal reference. Each two-year pair shares a release. Stats SA's intervening Q3 2024 Table B independently reproduces all 22 Q3 2023 and Q3 2024 cells in the collection at published precision. This provides a check across the two most recent paired vintages. The historical workbook remains unavailable, so revisions elsewhere and differences at finer precision remain unverified. Stats SA documents reweighting after revised population benchmarks. The extracts can support broad descriptive comparisons with the vintages shown. Small movements, particularly across vintages, warrant restraint. [2024 Table B](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0211/P02113rdQuarter2024.pdf#page=7) · [Stats SA survey reweighting documentation](https://isibaloweb.statssa.gov.za/pages/epdp.php)

QLFS is a weighted household survey. The relevant releases cover employed people aged 15–64, including private dwellings and workers' hostels. The release technical notes describe the sample design, non-response adjustments and calibration to population estimates. The 2020–2021 switch to telephone interviewing changed coverage and necessitated bias adjustments. Mainly in-person interviews resumed in 2022. The 2023 Q3 technical notes still describe raking to telephone non-coverage-adjusted control totals, while the 2025 Q3 notes describe calibrated household weights without that additional raking step. These changes matter for comparisons around 2020–2022. The 2025 questionnaire revision implemented revised international standards for work, employment relationships and informality. Stats SA reports continuity in employment and unemployment measurement and definitions. It flags a break in formal/informal categories. Broad Table B industry series remain usable with the questionnaire flag. [2021 technical notes, §§8.4–8.5](https://datafirst.uct.ac.za/dataportal/index.php/catalog/888/download/12154) · [2022 release, introduction](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0211/P02113rdQuarter2022.pdf#page=6) · [2023 technical notes, §§8.4–8.5](https://www.datafirst.uct.ac.za/dataportal/index.php/catalog/949/download/12570) · [2025 introduction and technical notes](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0211/P02113rdQuarter2025.pdf) · [Stats SA questionnaire clarification](https://www.statssa.gov.za/?p=18678)

For R, filter `observation_quarter == 3` and `geographic_scope == "national"`. Use one observation per `observation_period` × `industry_standardised`. Use `employment_persons` or the original thousand-person estimate with its unit. Keep `national_total` separate. The published totals are 3–15 thousand above the sum of the ten displayed industries because the totals include Other and estimates are independently rounded. Keep the published totals without deriving an Other residual. Carry `publication_vintage` and `comparability_flag`. The latter distinguishes telephone collection in 2020–2021, the 2022 return to mainly in-person collection, and the 2025 questionnaire change. For a strictly pre-announcement presentation, distinguish each row's source release from first known availability. For example, Q3 2024 appears in the collected November 2025 vintage and was already published in November 2024 with the same values. QLFS industries are contextual series and supply no denominator for CEE management or B-BBEE ownership. Avoid formal/informal comparisons across Q3 2025 and significance claims from these rounded point estimates alone.
