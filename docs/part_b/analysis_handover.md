# Part B analysis handover

## Reproduce the analysis

From the repository root, run:

```sh
Rscript --vanilla analysis/part_b/run_analysis.R
```

The entry point resolves the repository from its own path, so an absolute path works from another directory. It reads the reviewed CSVs under `data/part_b/processed/` and writes beneath `outputs/part_b`. The run is offline and uses R, `data.table`, `ggplot2` and Cairo PDF support. It was verified using R 4.3.3, data.table 1.18.2.1 and ggplot2 4.0.3. The entry point runs the analytical checks automatically and stops with the name of the first failing check. [Entry point](../../analysis/part_b/run_analysis.R) · [Session information](../../outputs/part_b/diagnostics/session_info.txt)

| Output path below `outputs/part_b` | Contents |

Analytical shares use the 0–100 scale. Columns ending in `_pp` report differences in percentage points. CSVs use `NA` for missing values. Original headings and source references are retained. Tables use shortened sector labels for display and preserve the full source wording.

## Selections and interpretation

- **CEE management.** Use count-based national observations from 2016–2024 and 18 detailed sectors from 2019–2024. Recompute and verify the two shares from the raw demographic cells. The black numerator is African, Coloured and Indian men and women, or women alone for the black female measure. The denominator is all managers at that level, including foreign nationals. The mapping uses exact headings in the reviewed 18-sector reference, an agriculture punctuation alias and the 2019 water/waste Appendix C heading. For that last alias, the ten printed demographic shares and reported 100% total match the water/waste rows in both body tables (20th report PDF pp. 28 and 31 versus p. 78). The annual mapping table records the original headings, years, pages and basis for linking. Historical 11-sector rows are outside the comparison, and the detailed labels do not establish stable employer composition. National EAP sums the reported Q3 demographic percentages as contextual benchmarks. The 2025 management file retains the changed-regime flag and stands on its own. [CEE review](cee_independent_review.md) · [Detailed mapping](../../outputs/part_b/tables/cee_sector_label_mapping.csv)
- **B-BBEE ownership.** Use `retro_2026`, 2016–2023, all entity sizes, and the four distinct published measures. The national rows are the Commission’s published averages. Sample counts describe context and have no indicator-denominator interpretation. The Property blank in 2013 remains missing outside the primary period. MAC has no 2016 observation. The sensitivity retains the source values and masks both rows of each affected female-overall pair in the excluded scenario. Eight of the ten pairs fall inside the period. Generic economic interest and Financial Services voting rights then lack a 2016 baseline. The table labels comparisons beginning in 2017 separately. The figure shows published observations and uses open markers and line breaks at affected points. The four comparison codes have usable fixed endpoints in both scenarios and at least 30 contextual entries at each endpoint. [B-BBEE review](bbbee_independent_review.md) · [Sensitivity](../../outputs/part_b/tables/bbbee_ownership_change_and_sensitivity.csv)
- **B-BBEE status.** Use 2016–2023 and omit the National 2018 and Financial Services 2016 distributions. A valid status total can differ from sample coverage. At level detail, a published dash remains missing, and calculated shares use only numeric counts. The Levels 1–4 summary infers zero only for a dash with a published zero share in an otherwise reconciled distribution. Levels 5–8 are published contributor categories. [Valid level distributions](../../outputs/part_b/tables/bbbee_compliance_levels_valid.csv)
- **Employment Equity.** Join the May 2023 national BLACK targets and the same gazette’s 2022 BLACK workforce profile one-to-one on sector ID, occupational level and gender. The signs reflect target minus observed share at the published tenth of a percentage point. Rank descending within occupation and gender, assigning tied ranks equally. The final April 2025 designated-group targets have different demographic coverage and are shown in a separate primary table. The supplementary CEE proxy sums African, Coloured and Indian men and African, Coloured, Indian and White women over the full occupational denominator. Qualifying citizenship and the disability union are unavailable from the CEE counts. No disability adjustment enters the proxy. Workforce year 2023 provides pre-announcement context, and workforce year 2024 is retrospective. [Supporting review](supporting_independent_review.md) · [Draft comparisons](../../outputs/part_b/tables/ee_draft_2023_target_shortfalls.csv) · [Final targets](../../outputs/part_b/tables/ee_final_2025_targets.csv)
- **QLFS.** Use Q3 2016–2025 Table B. Draw national employment from the published total and retain the ten broad industries on their published basis. Release vintages are mixed, survey collection changed in 2020–2022, and the 2025 questionnaire revision affects measures of formality. QLFS provides context using a household survey population. CEE and B-BBEE have their own denominators and sector classifications. [Supporting review](supporting_independent_review.md#qlfs-provenance-and-comparability)

## Findings and figure choices

National black representation increased by 7.9 percentage points in top management and 9.7 in senior management from 2016 to 2024. Black female shares gained 5.1 and 6.0 points. The national ownership averages increased by 24.08 points for black economic interest and 15.11 for black female economic interest. Black voting rights gained 26.46 points and black female voting rights 10.93. National ownership findings and all retained fixed-endpoint changes are identical after the anomaly exclusions. The largest total shortfalls against the 2023 proposals were 21.5 points in accommodation and food for top management and 20.8 in education for senior management. [Preliminary results](../../outputs/part_b/reports/part_b_preliminary_results.md)

The first figure shows the two occupational levels for both representation measures. The second shows the economic-interest paths and marks the flagged published observations. The third uses the contemporaneous draft gaps because they speak directly to one of Part A’s announcements. The results include a readable comparison of management changes in all 18 CEE sectors. The supporting tables retain the annual paths and all four ownership measures by B-BBEE code.

## Validation and independent review

The R run checks key uniqueness, exact analytical coverage, row and count reconciliation, percentage arithmetic, sector label matches, gazette page pairings, flags and exclusions, proxy denominators, ownership sensitivity and the data selected for figures. The run records the processed input fingerprints and checks that they are unchanged. [Validation log](../../outputs/part_b/diagnostics/validation_checks.csv) · [Input fingerprints](../../outputs/part_b/diagnostics/input_fingerprints.csv)

The revised run passed 89 automated checks. To independently verify the tables, run `python3 analysis/part_b/tests/verify_methodology.py` after the R entry point. It recalculates the principal results and sensitivities from the reviewed inputs, compares 2,876 keyed rows across the 23 tables, and writes the results under `diagnostics/independent_methodology/`. SHA-256 fingerprints matched for all 330 pre-existing Part A outputs and 60 Part B collection files.
