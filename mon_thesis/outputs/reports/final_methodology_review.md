# Independent re-review of Part A methodological corrections

**Review date:** 24 November 2026. **Scope:** five registered Part A events and the corrected [0,+1] descriptive event-study results. This review evaluates the working files, not an assertion of causal identification. Estimates and intervals below are conditional on the stated return, sample and bootstrap assumptions.

## 1. Overall verdict

**Ready for supervisor review.** The identified conversion and donor-mask defects are corrected, the primary p-values and intervals now use compatible orderings, and the reported estimates independently reconcile with source returns, fitted models and firm CARs. The evidence supports a descriptive, conditional presentation, not a thesis-final causal or shareholder-wealth claim. Significant work is not required before showing Monique these results, but the accessible report should carry the qualifications below prominently. This classification is deliberately short of “ready for thesis integration”: the supervisor should decide whether the price-return convention, selected sample and conditional inference answer the intended substantive question.

The principal uncertainty is scientific, not a newly proven programming defect: a pre-event residual bootstrap cannot know whether event-day volatility or correlations changed. The calibration shows potentially substantial over-rejection if they do. The tests and pointwise intervals should therefore be described as conditional, and the absence of Holm significance must not be described as evidence of no effect. No critical or major new coding defect was demonstrated in this re-review.

## 3. New findings

**Moderate — Conditional uncertainty is easy to overstate in a reader-facing report.** This is an inferential limitation, not a new implementation defect. The 2026 result is especially dependent on the pre-event volatility and correlation assumption: active-donor and 1.5× event-innovation scenarios each give raw p=.0502, compared with .0134 baseline. Neither scenario estimates the true event-day variance. A positive pointwise interval and the Holm result should be shown together with the overlap and market-stress evidence. Avoid declaring the interval unconditionally calibrated.

**Minor — Automated test runner has side effects.** `tests/testthat.R` writes test diagnostics and refreshes `methodological_corrections_report.md`; executing the requested suite in the working project refreshed those generated artifacts. It did not alter the source package, archived baseline, production code or statistical tables. Later checks used an isolated copy. Run future tests in a copy when strict output preservation is needed. This is an audit workflow issue, not an error in estimates.

**Minor — Monte Carlo variability is visible.** An independently coded 19,999-draw bootstrap on the 2018 baseline gives p=.1707 compared with the published 4,999-draw p=.1814. The difference is about 1.7 combined Monte Carlo standard errors (approximately .0061 using binomial approximations), consistent with different random streams; the estimates and substantive inference agree. This is not evidence that either calculation is exact. A larger draw count is optional for more stable displayed p-values, not a necessary correction.

No other new critical or major defect was established. Unverified return conventions, missing historic identities and conditional inference are not automatically coding errors.

## 5. Statistical and economic interpretation

All five point estimates are numerically reproducible conditional on the supplied data and classification. The 2017 and 2018 baseline mining spreads are negative but imprecise; the two EE aggregate estimates are close to zero with intervals covering economically relevant positive and negative values. No failure to reject establishes a zero effect. Classification changes tested to date do not reverse the mining baseline estimates, but missing historical classifications and uncertain legal exposure limit generalisation.

The 2018 negative gazette-window estimate is **not stable across distinct information dates**. The positive 21 September stimulus/approval window and negative 27 September gazette window have different news bundles and must not be averaged into a single causal narrative. For 2026, the positive same-date relative performance persists in several screens, but its magnitude changes with normal-return model and its inference changes with donor/volatility assumptions. Day +1 dominates and the raw portfolio fell over the two days. Neither result supports a policy-attribution or efficiency-cost claim. The benchmark incorporates affected firms and its fitted historical relationship is not a clean counterfactual for all contemporaneous shocks.

The available two event sessions cannot reliably identify a general event-period variance and correlation change. BMP/KP-style standardized CAR is an established sensitivity with scientific value, but its estimand differs from the raw equal-security CAAR and mining-control spread, and its assumptions do not guarantee robustness to changing common factors; the supplied simulation shows factor-change and skewness failures. It is appropriately exploratory. Promoting it or engineering a more elaborate model solely to change significance would not resolve the identification problem. Conditional inference, the existing explicit stress scenarios, and careful presentation are sufficient for supervisor review.
